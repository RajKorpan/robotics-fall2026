Part 1 · Why robotics software is difficult

Can the delivery robot stop safely?

You will run three small experiments with the same hospital delivery robot. Each experiment changes one part of the system and shows how that change affects the robot in the physical world.

🏥🤖

The situation

A delivery robot is traveling down a hospital hallway. A person steps into its path. To stop safely, the robot must detect the person, make a decision in time, send that decision through its software, and make its wheels respond as expected.

Example 1

The sensor’s report may differ from reality

The robot cannot directly know what is in the hallway. Its software acts using measurements sent by sensors.

NoiseReadings jump a little higher or lower each time, even when nothing moved.
BiasEvery reading is consistently too high or consistently too low.
DelayThe software receives an older measurement after the situation has already changed.
Missing measurementThe sensor sends no usable distance at all. It may be blocked, disconnected, or temporarily unable to detect the person. No measurement means the robot does not know whether the path is clear.

What to do

  1. Click Run normal measurement and watch where the robot stops.
  2. Change noise, bias, delay, or turn on a missing measurement.
  3. Click Run changed measurement and compare reality with the sensor report.
  4. Change the settings again and rerun if you want another comparison.

Example 2

A late stop command becomes extra travel

Imagine that a person steps out of a doorway. Time passes while the sensor detects them, software processes the measurement, a decision travels to the controller, and the wheels brake.

SenseThe sensor detects the person and reports a distance.
DecideThe software uses that distance to choose STOP.
ActThe software sends the stop command to the robot.

This sense, decide, and act loop must finish before the wheels begin responding. Delay anywhere in the loop increases the distance the robot travels.

What to do

  1. Click Run normal response to see a slow robot with a short response time.
  2. Increase speed or response time.
  3. Click Run changed response and compare the stopping position.
  4. Change the settings again and rerun if you want another comparison.

Example 3

The floor can change what the same wheel command does

Wheel slip happens when a wheel turns but does not grip the floor enough to move the robot as expected, like a car tire spinning on ice.

What to do

  1. Click Drive on dry floor. Both visible wheels grip the floor and the robot follows the expected straight path.
  2. Select Slippery floor under right wheel.
  3. Click Drive changed surface. Watch the right wheel spin and the robot turn away from the expected path.
  4. Run either condition again to replay it from the starting position.
