# Instructor notes

This lab deliberately uses explicit course requirements so students can see configurations pass and fail. Present those requirements as scenario constraints, not universal moral or technical truths.

Mission 1 models utility and residual privacy risk rather than claiming to measure them empirically. Mission 2 uses test conditions rather than demographic essentialism and assumes perfect review of abstained fixture cases; require students to challenge that assumption. Mission 3 uses simplified policy checks and is not a safety certification.

Grade traceability across four links: concern → engineering change → measured consequence → justified revision. A student should not pass by repeating principles without connecting them to exact settings and scenario evidence.

Ask who bears the cost of each intervention: people denied assistance, people incorrectly approached, reviewers handling uncertain cases, operators managing data, and users adapting to conservative robot behavior.
